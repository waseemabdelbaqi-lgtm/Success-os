Welcome to Success OS Academy. In this AP Physics 1 lesson, we will distinguish position, displacement, and velocity. Position tells us where an object is relative to a chosen origin. A positive or negative coordinate does not mean good or bad; it only shows direction on the coordinate axis. Displacement is the change in position. It equals final position minus initial position. Unlike distance, displacement depends only on the starting and ending positions. Average velocity equals displacement divided by elapsed time, so it includes direction. For example, an object moves from negative two meters to positive ten meters in four seconds. Its displacement is twelve meters, and its average velocity is positive three meters per second. On a position versus time graph, average velocity is the slope between two points. Instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent line at one instant. A steeper positive slope means a larger positive velocity. A horizontal line means zero velocity. A negative slope means motion in the negative direction. Remember: position is a location, displacement is a change in location, and velocity is the rate of change of position. Pause now and solve this challenge. A runner moves from six meters to negative eighteen meters in eight seconds. Find the displacement and average velocity, then explain the meaning of the signs. Check your work using both the equation and the slope of a position-time graph.
